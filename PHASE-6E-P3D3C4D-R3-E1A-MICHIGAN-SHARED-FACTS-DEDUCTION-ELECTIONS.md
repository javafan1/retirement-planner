# R3-E1A · Michigan deduction elections and shared retirement facts

## Decision and baseline

**BLOCKED_PENDING_APPROVAL.** The existing facts suffice for a conditional worksheet for a living original IRA owner. They do not establish the full choice of Michigan deductions for a certified production projection. The standard-deduction interaction with the §30(1)(e) deduction and the government employment exceptions are unrepresented. The state adapter also lacks a versioned Michigan calculation route for replay and omits the federally taxable Social Security amount from its internal base. Applying the change to all current Michigan callers would change legacy estimates. Stage A delivers the design and worksheet. Stage B requires approval of the bounded assumptions and historical policy. Michigan R3 scheduling remains gated.

Local `main` and its `origin/main` tracking ref were at `43f4dda9dd544bba58df5e95d401063291ebe645`. Accepted R2 and R3 source, test and report edits were present, including the earlier R2a3-B report edit. The index was empty. R3-E1 Stage A's accepted baseline is 5,804 passes, zero failures and errors, and 35 skips. The existing characterization test was retained. This phase changes only this report.

Reviewed R3-B through R3-E1, Michigan 6C1, 6C2 and 6C3, and the R1/R2 ownership, inherited employer, rollover and ordinary-withdrawal architecture. The field and consumer inventory follows current source.

**SHARED_FACT_MISSING.** No general employer mandated-versus-elective employee and employer contribution allocation, attributable earnings, original rollover composition, traditional IRA basis ledger, general pension public jurisdiction/reciprocity or Michigan-specific qualification evidence was found in these models. The nonqualified annuity taxable/basis evidence is limited to that receipt family. No stored state return evidence proves the §30(1)(e) interaction for military, railroad or other covered receipts, government employment not covered by Social Security, public safety classification, survivor prior-return deductions or no remarriage. The amount and COLA fields cannot substitute for these facts. Existing federal pretax assumptions remain modeling assumptions.

## Integration and historical policy design

**CURRENT_IMPLEMENTATION.** `TaxFundingCalculator` attaches actual or trial receipt sources, computes the federal result, and routes both to `StateTaxCalculationRequest`. `MichiganProductionTaxAdapter` passes only attributed income, filing status, Michigan rules and Michigan context to the legacy calculator. Michigan estimates use a separate ordinary-before-SS base, eligible-source subtotal, return-level cap and ordinary exemptions. All methods invoking state tax share the dispatcher. `CompleteProjectionSnapshot` has replay checks for scope-specific new policies, including R2/R3 and Arkansas, but no Michigan election identity or Michigan election replay selector. Merely adding a string to the PDF would not establish which calculation ran.

**REQUIRES_USER_APPROVAL.** Add a separate Michigan applied tax policy after the scope and assumptions are accepted. Keep an explicit legacy default for frozen history. Carry the selected policy via existing immutable annual inputs to initial, bracket-trial, gross-up, final settlement and Medicare retry. Compute candidates as pure functions of the actual trial graph; choose minimum valid tax with a deterministic tie rule that preserves the retirement path on a tie. Rerun the same policy independently on frozen inputs. Bind law version, residence, annual facts, source decisions, election and settled annual graph. Prefer existing provenance and retained fields unless independently replayable proof requires a schema addition. Preserve all old schema semantics, UUIDs, associations, fingerprints and disclosure. A rebuilt digest with forged annual deductions or payments must still fail replay. Version dispatch is required even if the first reported tax difference is small.

An applied identity solves replay dispatch after a result is accepted. Choosing when a live plan enters that policy is a separate decision. An automatic switch based on state and year would reinterpret existing saved plans that contain the same inputs. An opt-in with no normal workflow would leave the demonstrated defect in the production entry point. Approve the activation boundary before wiring either behavior. An initial policy can be bounded to fully represented ordinary 2026 living-owner receipts with exclusions; employer, pension and survivor qualification need not be prerequisites to that policy.
