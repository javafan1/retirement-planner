# Phase 6E-P3D3C4 — Arkansas death-year taxable receipt recipient audit

## Finding and native evidence boundary

The exact Arkansas message means a positive-gross receipt reached individual-recipient reconciliation carrying an owner or recipient that the annual tax context did not list as active. The message omits the receipt kind, payment purpose, and owner status. Three production-path synthetic cases reached it in 2056. Two drew from a deceased individual's ordinary IRA, one for spending and one for tax funding. The third used a pension attributed to `JOINT` while both individuals were alive. A forced annual calculation with both individuals deceased refused at a different, earlier filing-status check.

The reported native 2056 refusal has not been reproduced from the native input. The workspace's prior acceptance fixture is synthetic. No matching saved input, failing receipt evidence, or native debugger capture was available for this audit. The native receipt, exact trial, and relationship of 2056 to the first death remain unresolved. If the run was the normal controlled deterministic Arkansas Basic path, the plan could model at most one household death, so second death and the year after second death fall outside that route. Regular Tax Only exposes this independent source guard when its earlier low-income guard is omitted. Both policies rejected the same synthetic inactive-recipient draw. ## Modeling and legal boundary

An ordinary distribution attributed to a deceased owner in the modeled year cannot stand as that person's current receipt under the January 1 convention. Real-world death-year income can include amounts actually or constructively received before death. Unreceived amounts can require separate treatment for an estate or recipient, and inherited IRA and year-of-death RMD rules depend on the actual beneficiary, required beginning date and distributions already made. Arkansas DFA's 2025 instructions contemplate a deceased individual's return. They do not establish who received a post-death pension or distribution. None of these authorities certifies 2056 law or a new product rule. The project already supplies lifetime cutoff, direct survivor pension, supported inherited-account facts and qualified life receipt boundaries. No automatic post-death retitling or alternate payee is supported by these facts alone. [IRS Publication 559, final return and income in respect of a decedent](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p559) · [IRS Publication 590-B, inherited IRAs and death-year distributions](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p590b) · [Arkansas DFA 2025 AR1000F/AR1000NR instructions, “Deceased Taxpayer”](https://www.dfa.arkansas.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025_AR1000F_and_AR1000NR_Instructions.pdf)

## Root-cause classification and cross-state comparison

**Confirmed synthetic defect in source allocation.** In Arkansas, opening RMD, annual RMD and conversion already use annual owner eligibility. Ordinary spending and exact tax funding can allocate from the full projected portfolio with no equivalent owner-availability rule. The allocator attaches the real account ownership correctly. Reconciliation then correctly rejects it as a current individual receipt. This can surface immediately at the first death, in a later survivor year when cash runs out, or in a tax-funding trial after a valid conversion. Fixing receipt attribution alone would leave the unsupported cash movement in place. **Shared allocation hazard, different tax handling.** In the same inactive-IRA spending fixture Florida completed, reported the `$1,000` in federal AGI, and preserved the inactive owner's source in the annual ledger. The Basic federal calculator prices aggregate income without an Arkansas-style individual-recipient reconciliation.
