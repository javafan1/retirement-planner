# Phase 6E-P3D3C4B — Household asset commingling and survivor tax attribution audit

## 1. Executive summary

**Classification: IMPLICIT HOUSEHOLD POOLING ONLY.** Aledrix keeps both spouses’ investment balances in the projected household portfolio after the first modeled death. Ordinary spending and tax-funding paths can reach those balances. The debit keeps the account’s configured owner. No general death-triggered retitling, successor-recipient policy, spousal election, or beneficiary RMD schedule moves with the balance. RMDs and conversions apply their own owner eligibility, and state reconciliation can prevent actual use of available household balances.

The supplied native diagnostic resolves the open question from P3D3C4A. The failing 2056 receipt was an ordinary traditional IRA **spending** withdrawal attributed to the deceased primary while the spouse was the sole active filer. It failed during initial exact Arkansas pricing with zero additional tax funding. Changing the survivor Social Security election can change when pooled cash becomes insufficient and which source the allocator reaches. The failure establishes a mismatch between economic availability and tax-recipient evidence. It does not establish that the survivor lacks an actual right to the funds, nor does it establish a real-world beneficiary election.

The narrow proposed path is an Arkansas Basic planning rule for eligible surviving-spouse distributions from a demonstrably covered traditional IRA, with original lineage, separate recipient and explicit source status. Before implementation, approve the underlying assumption, legal treatment and proof scope. Continue the typed refusal for unresolved succession, and keep conversion and RMD authorization separate.

## 5. Federal results and state divergence

Federal Basic pricing rolls actual deferred debits into household ordinary income and then AGI, with retirement income and Social Security inclusion handled through the existing aggregates. The standard deduction, brackets and Medicare income facts use the annual filing result, not the individual ownership printed on each source line. Thus an inactive primary’s draw can raise the surviving spouse’s modeled Single tax without a lawful recipient being established. With the same taxable amount and filing inputs, relabeling only an independently valid receipt should not alter Basic federal arithmetic. Ownership or source-history rules that change a mandatory distribution, taxable part, penalty, or actual funded withdrawal can change it. The deterministic couple keeps the configured status in the first-death year, then uses Single. The participant is already inactive on January 1 of the death year. IRS final-return and qualifying surviving spouse rules are broader than this planning convention. ## 6. RMDs, conversions and proof implications

- Opening and annual RMDs use eligible owner sets. Owner RMDs use the configured person’s age and applicable prior-year account balances or evidenced opening distributions. The generic deterministic engine retains both household owners in this eligibility set even when the death view has one inactive. Arkansas controlled and independent lifetime-mode paths instead use alive owners. In the matched 2028 scenario with a $26,500 primary IRA, the generic Florida path distributes $1,000 using the deceased original owner’s would-be age 73 and divisor 26.5. Arkansas leaves the balance untouched and generates no owner RMD on it. Neither behavior models the beneficiary’s death-year or ongoing legal obligation. Manually entered inherited IRA classes do not participate in owner RMDs and no independent beneficiary schedule was found. [HouseholdRmdCalculator.java](src/main/java/com/daviddunn/retirementplanner/domain/rmd/HouseholdRmdCalculator.java) · [OpeningRmdCalculator.java](src/main/java/com/daviddunn/retirementplanner/domain/rmd/OpeningRmdCalculator.java) · [OwnerRmdCalculator.java](src/main/java/com/daviddunn/retirementplanner/domain/rmd/OwnerRmdCalculator.java)
- Fixed and targeted conversions observe the same owner scopes through their applicable path. Conversion chooses eligible owners and requires the source and Roth IRA destination under the same modeled owner. A legacy Florida fixed conversion can therefore run from the original deceased owner's own traditional IRA into that owner’s Roth IRA, with targeted conversion also observed. Controlled Arkansas excludes the dead owner in both pathways and independently certifies the active spouse’s $5,000 capacity. This is different from a rollover into the surviving spouse’s account. The actual age or funding source of the survivor does not override the owner gate. ## 10. Recommended modeling policy and Arkansas minimum correction

**User-stated intent.** Preserve household economic access to the first decedent’s investment assets for ordinary retirement planning. The user’s request authorizes audit only and leaves tax options, real beneficiary rights and specific succession assumptions open.

**Current implementation.** Keep January 1 effective death. Leave both sets of account balances in the projection, apply current ordering, retain original owner, and reconcile actual source evidence to active tax owners where required. Supported life and pension flows already have their own payee semantics. Current modeled owner RMD and conversion behavior varies by execution route.

**Proposed new rule for approval.** Choose B with Arkansas Basic-only admission, initially for post-death ordinary spending and tax-funding from fully pretax ordinary traditional IRAs under an explicitly accepted and verified surviving-spouse treatment. Define whether the approximation represents a qualifying spouse-as-owner treatment or an actual beneficiary distribution, then certify only that treatment. These options can produce different age, retirement-pool and RMD results. The narrow candidate is a documented spouse-as-owner-for-ordinary-distributions assumption for cases where available inputs establish eligibility, the applicable RMD and source-basis conditions are within scope, and the sole living modeled spouse is the eligible recipient. Preserve the original position and ledger debit, with a separate effective payee and dated transaction evidence. Allow retirement exclusion only on independently reviewed facts. Same-death-year transfers, leftover decedent RMD, successor RMD required but unmapped, early/basis/penalty issues, multiple-beneficiary or contested status should retain typed refusal.
